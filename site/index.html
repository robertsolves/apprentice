Human pace · Real cursor and keys · You approve

Your agent on Mac, Linux and the web, like a person

apprentice lets a coding agent or a hosted model use macOS, Linux and the web the way a normal person would, not a bot: the real cursor on a curved path, real keys at a typing rhythm, pauses to look, and rests between bursts. It reads the screen as a short numbered list and proposes every change before it makes it.

What you can ask

Anything you'd do yourself with the mouse and keyboard, one task at a time. Say it to your agent in plain words.

“Download last month's invoice from the Stripe dashboard and put it in ~/Documents/Invoices.”
“Reply to the latest email from my accountant in Mail: I'll send the receipts on Friday.”
“Book the 7am spin class on my gym's website for Thursday.”
“Go through my LinkedIn notifications and tell me which ones need a reply.”
“Fill in the contact form on the council website with my details from contacts.md.”
“Turn on Night Shift from 10pm in System Settings.”
“Post this changelog to the #updates channel in our Discord.”
“In the Linux VM, open the report in LibreOffice and save it as a PDF.”
“Do the weekly Shopify stock check like last time.”

How it works

One Swift binary, CLI and daemon in one, with no package dependencies. You never type its commands: your agent runs them from its own shell or over MCP, and the daemon does the reading and the careful acting.

Acts like a person

Curved pointer paths with overshoot, a natural key rhythm with the odd correction, pauses before it acts and rests between bursts. Sites see a careful human pace, not a script hammering at them.

Reads text, not pixels

The accessibility tree becomes a numbered list of what's on screen. A step costs tens of tokens, not a screenshot. shot and ocr are there for the rare page that hides.

Real input only

Every click, scroll and keystroke goes through the system's own input stream with the window in front. Nothing is injected into pages and nothing is pressed through accessibility.

Proposes, then acts

Every change is a proposal first. Nothing happens until it's approved, and your agent checks with you on anything risky: send, delete, buy, passwords, settings.

Asks you

For decisions that are yours, a little window opens at the top of the screen: free text, choices, or "point at it". When you're away, the question goes back to the chat.

Stops for you

It waits until you've been idle. Touch a key or the mouse and it stops at once and leaves the cursor where you are. ctrl+opt+cmd+. is a kill switch.

Site rules built in

Per-site budgets, cooldowns and allowlists keep the account well inside what you could do by hand. A CAPTCHA or checkpoint goes to you, never around you. Sites whose terms forbid automation (Meta, TikTok) are refused.

Learns as it goes

Notes about apps and sites live in the repo. A task that worked becomes a flow your agent can rerun next time, with one approval for the whole plan.

Remembers your usual

Tell your agent "my gym is …" once and it saves it. Next time it goes straight there. Each VM keeps its own preferences and inherits your Mac's, and only your own words or the sites you used get saved.

Fixes and updates itself

It runs from your own clone. Fixes are small commits that rebuild, self-test and restart. Upstream updates are rebased in, and a failed build keeps the old binary.

A Linux VM on the side

A small VM runs Chromium and any Linux app with its own screen, cursor and keyboard, so the agent works while you keep working. One VM per job, run side by side.

Built for one person

apprentice is for a solo owner running a handful of tools and accounts at a pace they could keep up by hand. It's meant to bring the care of a bigger team, not more volume. Bulk outreach, mass following, scraping at volume and running several accounts on one site are out of scope on purpose.

Install

You need macOS 14 or later and Swift (Xcode or the command-line tools). One command installs it, and running it again updates:

git clone https://github.com/robertsolves/apprentice.git ~/.apprentice/src && ~/.apprentice/src/install.sh
  1. It builds a release binary, runs its self-test and installs it to ~/.local/bin/apprentice, signed so your permissions survive rebuilds.
  2. It connects every AI agent it finds on your Mac, so you can just ask it to do things on screen.
  3. Grant Accessibility and Screen Recording to ~/.local/bin/apprentice in System Settings → Privacy & Security, then run apprentice stop.
